The Eight-Game Teaching Ladder

For brand-new players: eight tiny games, one idea per game. Each idea joins the board only after the last one worked — by Game 8 they’re playing real chess.

# On the board The one idea How the host teaches it Done when…
1 8 pawns vs 8 pawns. First pawn to the last rank wins the game. Pawns step one square (two on the first go) but capture diagonally. Play a bogus move once on purpose, so they get to catch you. Ask: “Which of my pawns can take yours next turn?” they win (or survive) a pawn race and have promoted at least one pawn.
2 Add the two kings. King is attacked = you lose. Kings can never stand next to each other — the two thrones need a one-square gap. March your king adjacent to theirs once; let them say “no, you can’t!” Then show how the king guards your pawns. they win the pawn-plus-kings game — by promotion, not by accident.
3 Add both rooks (back corners). Rooks love open files — a column with no pawns is a rook highway. Open one column clear of pawns, park your rook there, and let them feel the long reach. Point at the highway metaphor. they use an open file to win a pawn or trap a piece.
4 Add both bishops. Bishops are married to their color: one only ever stands on light squares, its twin on dark, forever. Trace a full diagonal to the board edge with your finger. Then ask: “Which of my pieces can this bishop NEVER reach?” they trap or capture a piece with a diagonal nobody saw coming.
5 Add both knights. Knights hop in an L, leap over anyone, and always land on the other color. Let them use the knight to jump your pawn wall in one move. That leap is the “aha” — pause and enjoy it. they fork two of your pieces with one knight (even dunking your rook counts).
6 Add both queens. Full army, no castling yet. The queen is the whole board’s strongest piece — and the easiest one to lose. Keep her home a few moves. Lose your queen early on purpose, once, and say “see? Even queens get greedy.” The lesson sticks better than any lecture. they finish a full-army game without losing the queen for nothing.
7 Full chess, castling now legal. A king in the middle is a target; castling tucks him in behind a pawn wall — aim to do it early. Draw the little two-square jump with the rook remodel. Say: “first job of the game: where does my king sleep?” they castle in a real game without needing to be reminded.
8 Real chess. Full board, full rules. Checkmate ends the game — not eating every piece. Careful: no legal moves but no check is a draw (stalemate). Show one clean mate-in-one, then one stalemate side by side. Both look like wins; only one is. they win or draw a full first game — and stamp the last box of their Progress Passport.

Host’s pacing note: one ladder game per visit is plenty. Play the day’s game twice if they’re hungry — repeat it rather than rush ahead. Ladder games are won by promotion, capture-all, or a quick mate-on-the-king; any ending you both agree to keep short is legal at this table.

Matching with ladder students: beginners play beginners in the ladder games; graduates of Game 8 join the main pairing grid. Both groups earn stamps — the passport doesn’t care which table you sat at.
